# Optional synthetic examples (not a phrase bank)

**Everything here is invented for demonstration.** The people, jobs and results are fictional. Never reuse them in a real user's message, and never treat this file as a default template or a job-to-experience mapping. Study *why* a piece of evidence was chosen; do not copy sentence patterns.

## Example A · Design (synthetic)

**JD excerpt**: Improve the usability of complex workflows through user research and prototype testing.

**Candidate facts**: Ran user interviews and usability tests for a multi-step interface, then redesigned the flow based on findings. No quantified conversion data.

**Why this evidence**: The job is about finding interaction friction and validating fixes. The evidence contains the same diagnosis and testing actions, so there is no need to invent growth numbers.

**Example message**: Hi, I recently led usability work on a complex multi-step flow: interviews surfaced where people got stuck, and I iterated the design through prototypes and usability tests. I'd be glad to hear which interaction problems your team is tackling. Open to a quick chat?

## Example B · Marketing (synthetic)

**JD excerpt**: Find growth opportunities, design experiments and explain campaign results.

**Candidate facts**: Led one content-promotion experiment, testing channels and creatives in batches with documented results. No confirmed overall revenue impact.

**Why this evidence**: It uses completed experiment design and analysis without inflating channel performance into business revenue.

**Example message**: Hi, I led a content-promotion experiment where audience insights set the hypotheses, then we compared real responses across channels and creatives to shift spend. Happy to walk through how we designed and reviewed it. Would a short call work?

## Example C · Product (synthetic)

**JD excerpt**: Spot automation opportunities in repetitive work and independently build prototypes that can be validated.

**Candidate facts**: Independently built an internal prototype for a team workflow and refined key steps from user trials. Not yet in production.

**Why this evidence**: It shows hands-on validation without claiming production-grade automation.

**Example message**: Hi, I turned a workflow my team kept repeating into a prototype people could try, then adjusted the interaction and steps based on how they actually used it. I'd like to bring that quick-validation habit to new product problems. Could we talk?

## Example D · Cross-industry transfer (synthetic)

**JD excerpt**: Own end-to-end quality of an AI search experience, from query understanding through ranking to answer generation; define what a good result is and build evaluation sets.

**Candidate facts**: In a previous role in a different industry, owned a recommendation product end to end: parsing user needs, filtering and ranking candidates, and having a language model write the reason for each recommendation. Built the evaluation set and rubric used to iterate it.

**Why this evidence**: The industries differ, but the mechanism (understand the need → retrieve and rank → generate an explanation) and the evaluation ownership are the same. The message describes the work in search terms and leaves out the old industry's jargon, without claiming search experience.

**Example message**: Hi, I'm a product manager who owned a recommendation product end to end: understanding what users asked for, ranking candidates, and having the model explain each pick, plus the evaluation set and rubric we iterated against. Your search team's work from query understanding to answer generation is the same kind of problem. Open to a chat?

## Don't learn the wrong lesson

- No form of address or sign-off in these examples is preferred.
- The examples do not license moving one person's achievements to another candidate.
- Two jobs in the same profession do not automatically call for the same example or phrasing.
